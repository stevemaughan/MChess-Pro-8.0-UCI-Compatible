# Deviations from the original

Every behavioural difference between the rewrite and the original MChess Pro 8 is listed here (AGENTS.md §2.5, §9). Status is one of:

- **candidate**: found while reading the original; the class and the fix are not yet agreed;
- **planned**: agreed, not yet implemented;
- **implemented**: in the code, with a test;
- **pending decision**: needs the project owner.

The rewrite began on 2026-11-18 (`docs/PORT_STATUS.md`). An entry is "implemented" only for the units already ported.

## DEV-001: The hash key is updated on the wrong square for en passant

- **Class:** C (logic bug). **Status:** implemented, 2026-11-18. The owner decided on 2026-11-17 that it is to be corrected.
- **Original:** `update_hash_key` (`4793:3EA2`), at `3F70`–`3F81`. For an en-passant capture it removes the captured pawn from the keys at the square "from ± 10". The captured pawn stands on "to ∓ 10". The two are different squares.
- **Effect:** after any en-passant move in the search, both the position hash key (`DG:8CCC`) and the pawn hash key (`DG:8CD2`) are wrong for that line. A position reached through en passant is stored and probed under a key that does not match the same position reached another way. Moves played in the game are not affected for long: after a game move the keys are recomputed from scratch (`4793:4026`).
- **Evidence of intent:** every other kind of capture removes the captured piece from the keys at the square where it stands. In 191 traced en-passant moves both keys were wrong afterwards; no other kind of move left them wrong (`docs/arch/20-position-make-unmake.md`).
- **Trigger:** any en-passant capture inside the search tree.
- **Fix:** remove the captured pawn at "to ∓ 10": to − 10 when White captures, to + 10 when Black captures. Switchable, as every class C fix is (AGENTS.md §9.2). In the rewrite it is the compile-time `Fixes` parameter of `Position.updateHashKey` in `src/position.zig`: `Fixes.original` reproduces the original and is what the replay of recorded calls uses; `Fixes.corrected` is the fix. In the model the switch is `FIX_DEV001_EP_HASH` in `tools/research/position/model.py`, off by default so that the model still matches the original.
- **Behaviour change where the original worked:** none outside lines containing an en-passant capture.
- **Test:** `tools/research/position/test_dev001.py`. For a White and a Black en-passant capture it shows the original update leaving both keys different from the keys of the resulting position, and the corrected update leaving them equal; for an ordinary pawn capture the two agree. The same test exists in the rewrite, in `src/position_test.zig`, and passes.

## DEV-002: The per-ply arrays are not bounds-checked

- **Class:** A (crash). **Status:** implemented, 2026-11-18, as a bound; the reachable maximum ply is still to be proved.
- **Original:** `make_move` (`1E9D:000C`) writes per-ply records (en-passant square, check flag, reversible-move flag, quiet-move flag) indexed by ply with no check. By reading, the en-passant record at ply 46 would overwrite the search board, and the quiet-move record at ply 40 would overwrite the "pondering" flag at `DG:3676`.
- **Evidence so far:** read from the code, not triggered. The deepest ply seen in any run was 23. The iteration loop caps the ply ceiling at 35 (`docs/arch/31-root-iteration-time.md`), which may keep the search clear of both, but extensions and quiescence have not been shown to respect it.
- **Fix:** in the rewrite all four per-ply arrays hold 64 plies (`max_ply` in `src/position.zig`), above the search's ply ceiling of 35 and the deepest ply seen, 23. A ply of 64 or more is a checked error in safe builds. Still to do: prove from the search that no path exceeds the ceiling, or bound it there.

## DEV-063: The move generators write past the 100 entries of a move list

- **Class:** A (memory corruption). **Status:** implemented, 2026-11-21 (task capacity). No switch: class A fixes have none; `mchess` and `mchess-original` both have it.
- **Original:** the list of ply p is `DG:3A34 + 400·p`, 100 moves of 4 bytes, and no generator tests the count while it writes (`docs/arch/21-move-generation.md`, §3 item 13 and open question 3). `gen_all_moves` (`1E9D:3938`) writes its scored moves (captures that do not lose, marked moves, the queen's checks, threatening pushes, promotions, castling) into the list and its unscored moves from `DG:B106` on, 4 bytes each; at its end (`48D8`–`492F`) it sorts the scored group, caps the unscored copy at `100 − n1` and returns n1 + n2. Traced at the root of `R6R/3Q4/1Q4Q1/4Q3/2Q4Q/Q4Q2/pp1Q4/kBNN1KB1 w` (218 moves, the most a legal position has) and of its mirror for Black: n1 = 9 (`[bp-2]` at `48E8`), 209 unscored (DX at `4909`), 91 copied (DX at `492A`), AX = 100. The 100 kept are the 9 scored ones sorted and the first 91 unscored ones in the order generated [traced: the port with that rule gives the original's root search, line hash included]. Before the cap the original has written all 209 unscored moves, `B106`–`B449`: the 201st and later lie over the castling stamps `B426` (White O-O) and `B428` (Black O-O) and over `B42A`–`B449` (`B42B` is the PV length). Traced at both 218-move roots, with a read hook on `B426`–`B46F` from that call on: the search reads two of the damaged words, the castling stamps, in eval_pieces_and_kings (`0EF8:0242` `[B426]`, `0EF8:0CC8` `[B428]`, each `cmp word, 0`); after the search update_castle_stamps (`38C0:0130`, `0158`), the from-scratch hash key (`4793:40E6`, `4108`) and the book's move check (`4793:58DE`, `5946`) read them, and the game-record code reads `B42A`, the set-up side (`38C0:121E`, `133D`, `1469`). `B42B` is written again (`0000:13F0`) before anything reads it, and nothing read `B42C`–`B449`. In those searches both stamps were already FFFFh (lost) and stay non-zero after the damage (`B426` becomes a from and a to byte, `B428` FF00h: its flag byte is written 0 and the score byte `B429` not at all), so no decision changed. They would differ where White's O-O right is held (`B426` = 0: the damage makes it read as lost) or Black's was lost at a game ply below 256 (`B428` = 00xxh: the damage makes it 0, held) [read; no such position constructed]. If the scored group passes 100 it runs on into the next ply's list (dead at that moment; its node regenerates it before reading); then `100 − n1` is negative and the copy count wraps, `rep movsw` of about 32,760 words over the data segment from `B106` [read; not seen]. The four quiescence generators (`1E9D:579A`, `25A0`, `0EF8:9528`, `1E9D:4954`) have no cap at all: past 100 they write into the next ply's list and return the full count, the node sorts the whole list (`0000:4BBC`, `6142`) and then reads moves past the 100th from the next ply's list after its children have generated into it. No legal position was found that takes them past 100 (the most constructed was 85); In the traced searches below (5.1 million nodes) no quiescence list passed 36. `gen_evasions` (`38C0:E260`) cannot reach it: it writes only moves onto the line of the check or to the checker's square, and king moves: at most 8 for the king, 8 for each of the at most 2 pawns that can take the checker and 4 for every other pawn, 4 for a queen, 3 for a rook or a bishop, 2 for a knight: 68 for a side's 16 men.
- **Seen in the port before the fix:** ReleaseSafe panics in `addUnscored` (index 100, `SearchVars.unscored`); ReleaseFast wrote the unscored moves over the fields after `SearchVars.unscored` (the generators' hint squares) and on into the struct that holds it, so the UCI legality check, whose list follows its `SearchVars`, refused the legal mate c4a2 and played d2b2, and another run ended in an access violation. With the code before the fix, the replay test of `tests/golden/manymoves.jsonl` panics in ReleaseSafe (index 100 of 100) and ends in an access violation in ReleaseFast.
- **Trigger:** a position with more than 100 pseudo-legal moves for the side to move, at the root or at any full-width node; more than 200 for the damage at `B426`.
- **Fix** (`src/movegen.zig`): no generator writes past the 100th entry. Before each piece (one test a piece, none a move: a piece writes at most 27 entries) `Out.room` checks whether the list could pass 100; if so the moves so far move to a buffer of the call (`max_written` = 16 slots × 27) and the rest are written there (`Out.widen`); in `gen_all_moves` the test is `GenAll.uroom`, one test of both groups together (a piece adds at most 27 to their sum), which widens both, the unscored group starting from a copy of all 100 entries of `SearchVars.unscored` so that the score bytes the original leaves there come back unchanged. At the end (`Out.narrow`, `GenAll.unarrow`) the moves come back into the list. Where the original's result is defined, it is kept: `gen_all_moves` with n1 ≤ 100 returns exactly the original's list and count, and `SearchVars.unscored` holds the first 100 unscored moves; with n1 > 100 the list is the first 100 of the scored group after the original's own sort of all of it (`48F1`), which is the state just before the copy wraps, nothing is copied, and the count is 100, the original's AX ((100 − n1) + n1). A quiescence generator past 100 sorts all its moves with `sort_moves`, as the node then does, keeps the first 100 and returns 100; the node's own sort of a sorted list leaves it unchanged. The moves past the 100th, which the original reads from the next ply's list after it has been overwritten, are not searched. The stray writes at `B296`–`B449` are not made: the castling stamps and the variables after them keep their values. When a piece can take the enemy king (FFFFh) the moves written so far are put back as far as the list holds them, as before. `gen_evasions` is unchanged (its list stays bounds-checked in the safe builds). Cost (`bench: movegen`, the code before and after alternately in one session on a quiet machine, several rounds): gen_all_moves about 223 ns a call before and 236 after (+6 %, not explained: removing the per-piece test does not change it); the four quiescence generators within 1 to 2 %, either way. In whole searches no difference shows: `mchess-original` at the three positions of the node-count check (startpos depth 8, a Sicilian depth 7, a rook ending depth 9), old and new builds alternately, three rounds: 6.07 s and 6.12 s on average, with a spread of 2 % between rounds of one build.
- **Behaviour change where the original worked:** none. No recorded search changes (every golden and replay test passes unchanged); the five searches of `tests/golden/manymoves.jsonl` equal the original's: the 218-move roots, and two searches of 1.7 and 3.4 million nodes whose full-width nodes at plies 2 to 10 have up to 124 moves (unscored groups of up to 108). Where the original reads a damaged byte the port reads the undamaged one: in the recorded searches the same decisions (above); in a position of the two kinds above with more than 200 unscored moves at a node, the port keeps the castling right as the game left it where the original would turn it lost or held (not constructed).
- **Test:** `src/movegen_cap_test.zig`: every generator at the cap, against a reference without one (the generator run slot by slot): the 218-move position and its mirror for gen_all_moves (the 100 kept, the count, `SearchVars.unscored` and the fields after it), the same with every quiet move marked (a scored group of 218), and a state with one knight in 15 slots that takes eight rooks and queens for each of the five generators, both colours; guards before and after the list stay untouched; and gen_evasions on a large line check (30 moves, within its bound). `src/perft_test.zig` now requires that no node of its 743 positions has a full list (none has). `src/adapt_setup_manymoves_test.zig`: every search of `tests/golden/manymoves.jsonl` from the engine's own set-up, equal on move, nodes, makes and line hash. `src/engine_manymoves_test.zig`: the 218-move position and its mirror through UCI at depths 2 and 4 on both engines: c4a2 (c5a7) after 9 nodes, and no `illegal move` line.

## DEV-064: A FEN with the side to move in a check no legal move could have given is rejected

- **Class:** E (unsupported input), whose consequence was class A (a crash). **Status:** implemented, 2026-11-22: first for two pawns (the stress audit, `docs/ROBUSTNESS.md`, fault 1), then widened to the general rule by the owner's decision 38 (2026-11-22). No switch.
- **Original:** Setup mode (`38C0:6542`) tests only the side not to move, so it accepts a board on which the side to move is in any check at all, two pawns' included. The search's check value (`DG:8C06` + ply) names one checker, and the evasions (`38C0:E260`) leave the other pawn's check. That cause is read from the listing, not traced.
- **Seen (the origin):** `Q1b5/4q3/3PR3/2p3r1/8/8/1kp1p3/3KN2n w - - 0 1` with `go movetime 100`. The ReleaseSafe build stops with index 124 of 120 in `leaf.zig` `endgameKing` (a king square off the board); the ReleaseFast build dies with an access violation. Another such position gave an illegal first move in a reported line (d4e2). Of 150 random such positions, 13 failed (11 crashes, 2 illegal lines).
- **The rule:** if the side to move is in check, the position is accepted only if some single move of the other side can be taken back so that, before it, the side to move was not in check. The moves taken back are every move of chess: a piece back along its own movement (leaving its square empty or holding a man it took), a pawn's step, double step or capture, a promotion with or without a capture, an en-passant capture, and castling (the king then not in check and not crossing an attacked square). A FEN's en-passant square fixes the last move as that double step. Nothing else is asked of the earlier position (that it can be reached, that the other king was safe, that the material allows the man taken back), so no position a game reaches is refused.
- **Trigger:** two pawns' check; a pawn and a knight; two knights; three checkers; two sliders on one line through the king; a pawn on its own 2nd rank giving check; a single check that no move or discovery explains; a check already there before the double step a FEN's en-passant square names; a castling that explains the check only by castling out of or through check. Of 250,000 random placements with the side to move in check (2 to 12 men a side), 78,924 are refused.
- **Accepted:** every check a move gives, among them a double check by a discovering move, two rooks from a capturing promotion (d7xc8=R with the king on d8 and a rook on d1), a knight promotion giving check, castling giving check, and en passant giving a double check or a check only en passant explains.
- **Fix:** `src/fen.zig` `checkConsistency` and `checkExplained`, error `ImpossibleCheck` (it replaces `DoublePawnCheck`). The engine prints `info string error: position rejected: the side to move is in check in a way no legal move could have given` and answers `go` with `bestmove 0000`.
- **Behaviour change where the original worked:** the original's Setup mode accepted such a board; the engine refuses it. Among the committed tests, perft position 4 (`r3k2r/Pppp1ppp/1b3nbN/nP6/BBP1P3/q4N2/Pp1P2PP/R2Q1RK1 w kq -`, in `tests/golden/perft/perft.jsonl`, `src/helpers_test.zig` and `tools/scripts/uci_stress.py`), its mirror, two positions of `perft.jsonl` with bishop and rook checking (`4k3/8/8/8/1b6/8/8/4K2r w`, `4k2R/8/8/1B6/8/8/8/4K3 b`) and one of `src/eval_mirror_test.zig` (DEV-058) are such positions; those tests do not go through the FEN reader and are unchanged.
- **Measured:** with the refusal bypassed in a scratch build (ReleaseSafe), 2,000 refused random positions that Setup accepts, each searched for 100 ms: one crash (a two-pawn check; 11 of the 2,000 were two pawns), no hang, no illegal `bestmove`, `pv` or `currmove` in the other 1,989 (1 to 5 checkers).
- **Test:** `src/fen_test.zig`: "a check no legal move could have given is rejected" (20 positions, every family above), "a check a legal move can have given is accepted" (15), and "every position of tests/golden/fen/reachable_checks.txt is accepted" (5,000 positions after a legal move, every rare kind 250 times or more). Proved beyond them (2026-11-22, `tools/research/fen/impossible_check.py`): none of 275,813 positions with the side to move in check after a legal move (random games and constructions) is refused; on those and on 250,000 random placements in check, the rule and an independent brute-force judge (every man of the other side put back on every square, with every kind of man taken, python-chess deciding legality) agree on every position. `tools/scripts/uci_protocol_test.py`, session "rejected positions" (two pawns, a pawn and a knight, two knights); `tools/scripts/uci_stress.py`, whose model has its own copy of the rule.
## DEV-065: A long search grew the heap without bound (the rewrite's own fault)

- **Class:** A (memory exhaustion). **Status:** implemented, 2026-11-22.
- **Original:** none. This is not a behaviour of MChess Pro 8: the fault was in the rewrite's own join of the root and the search, in the tree since the first commit. The root's calls into the search (`src/engine_root.zig` `callNode`, `ply2`) gave the node context a list on the heap, and the context appended every node entry and every make of the root move's subtree to it (`src/engine_state.zig` `event`). The root read the list only when the root move returned, to count nodes and makes, keep the deepest ply and feed the hash of the line, and kept its memory afterwards.
- **Trigger:** any long search. `go infinite` after 1.e4: 2,181 MB of private bytes at 59 s, 4,986 MB at 110 s, 3,116 MB kept after `stop`. Eight engines searching reached 9.5 GB each and two died. A 32-bit build cannot survive it. The tests and the fast safety games never showed it, because their searches are short.
- **Fix:** the root's wrapper of the search (`StopCtx` `child` and `applyMove`) counts each node entry and each make, and feeds the line hash, as it happens (`countNode`, `countMake`). The list is removed; the node context's event list exists in test builds only. The loop's view (`rs.RootState`) is on the stack, not taken from the allocator. A search allocates nothing.
- **Behaviour change where the original worked:** none. Moves, node and make counts, the line hash, the trace and every `info` line apart from `time` and `nps` are unchanged (every golden test; 28 UCI game checks, 5,544 `bestmove` and 39,830 `info` lines, before and after). Private bytes during a search are flat: 311.4 MB through 120 s of `go infinite` (221 million nodes).
- **Test:** `src/engine_alloc_test.zig`: no allocation, resize or free between a search's start and its end, over searches of 0.9 to 3 million nodes. Before the fix it fails in the first search with 22 allocations (45,224,980 bytes). `docs/ROBUSTNESS.md`, "Fault 3".
- **Not done:** the stress harness has no memory check that would have caught this (a long `go infinite` with private bytes sampled); and a tablebase file that could not be unpacked at `uci` is still unpacked inside a probe, the one way left for a search to allocate.

## DEV-066: The legality guard refused every legal reply to check when the side to move had more than 100 pseudo-legal moves (the rewrite's own fault)

- **Class:** B (missing move; legal moves refused). **Status:** implemented, 2026-11-22 (owner decision 41; found by an outside review, `docs/code review astra.md`, finding 1). No switch. Not a behaviour of the original: the fault was in the rewrite's own legality test (DEV-051, DEV-049, position input), which the search never reads.
- **Original:** the root (`0000:0827`–`0843`), the move entry (`check_entered_move`, `38C0:5974`–`5995`) and the search's nodes generate with `gen_evasions` (`38C0:E260`) when the side to move is in check, and with `gen_all_moves` (`1E9D:3938`), whose list keeps 100 moves (`1E9D:4903`–`4913`; DEV-063), otherwise. `gen_evasions` writes at most 68 moves, so every reply to check is in its list.
- **The fault:** every legality test of the rewrite generated with `gen_all_moves` at every ply, in check too. With the king in check and more than 100 pseudo-legal moves, the king's moves and the interpositions fell past the cap. The PV test (`PvCheck.legalPrefix`) cut every line after its first move; the guard on the move handed back (§9.3 item 3) refused the search's legal move, its fallback (`firstLegalRootMove`) found none, and the engine printed `info string no move` and `bestmove 0000` with five legal moves on the board; position input (`Engine.findLegal`, both rebuild paths) refused every reply; the book's test (`bookMoveLegal`) had the same rule (no effect: no book position is in check with 100 moves). A move entered on the continuation path (`check_entered_move`) was accepted, since that path already used `gen_evasions`, so one move was accepted on one path and refused on another. The PV test also generated with every class C fix off (`genAllMoves`), so under DEV-007 its 100 for Black could differ from the corrected root's.
- **Trigger:** `R7/4Q3/6Q1/2Q5/8/5Q2/p2n4/k4K2 w - - 0 1` (107 pseudo-legal moves, 5 legal; a knight's check): `go depth 1` gave `bestmove 0000`; each of f1e1, f1e2, f1f2, f1g1, f1g2 was refused as input. Its colour mirror likewise. `R7/4Q3/6Q1/2Q5/8/5Q2/1k6/r4K2 w - - 0 1` (a rook's check): f1e2, f1f2 and f1g2 refused (the interpositions and a8a1 were in the first 100). `position fen R7/4Q3/6Q1/2Q5/8/5Q2/p7/kn3K2 b - - 0 1 moves b1d2 f1g2` refused; its search reported `pv b1d2` alone.
- **Fix** (`src/legality.zig`, one helper for all four tests): a move is legal when it is in the list of the generator the original uses there, `gen_evasions` (`helpers.genEvasions`) when the position's check record is set, else `gen_all_moves` with its cap, generated with the build's own class C fixes as the root and the nodes generate (`legality.generate`); a castling move is not made in check; and the mover's king is not attacked after the move (`legality.makeIfKingSafe`). The check record of the root is the byte main reads from the game record at `0000:0693` (`5A7B:0B54` + `0CBh`·side + move number, beside the en-passant byte at `0687`; `legality.rootRecord`); at later PV plies it is `check_by_ply[ply]`, which `make_move` writes (`093C`–`0953`). `PvCheck.load` reads both bytes of the root; `PvCheck.legalPrefix` (the PV, the guard and its fallback), `Engine.findLegal` and `EngineRoot.bookMoveLegal` (whose root byte main has already set at `0693`, before the book probe at `070F`) generate through the helper. Everything runs on copies, outside the search.
- **Kept, by the owner's decision:** outside check, with more than 100 pseudo-legal moves, a legal move past the cap is still refused on input, as the original's own move entry refuses it: at `R6R/3Q4/1Q4Q1/4Q3/2Q4Q/Q4Q2/pp1Q4/kBNN1KB1 w - - 0 1` (218 legal moves) the same 100 moves are accepted as before.
- **Behaviour change where the original worked:** none. The searches are untouched: A1 and its mirror give 583 and 874 nodes at depth 1, 9,049 and 8,682 at depth 3, as before; the five node counts of the UCI check are unchanged; every golden test passes.
- **Test:** `src/engine_legality_test.zig`: A1 and A2 at depths 1 and 3 (a legal bestmove, no "no move", the node counts above, A1's line at depth 3 longer than its first move: `f1e2 d2c4 c5c4 a1b2 f3c3`), the original's engine on both; every legal reply of A1, A2 and A4 accepted as input; `moves b1d2 f1g2` accepted after F, and F at depth 3 reporting `b1d2 f1f2 d2e4 g6e4 a1b2 f3c3` (5,466,194 nodes, as before); the 100 moves accepted at the 218-move root, the same as before. **The list-identity test:** in every test build, at every root generation (`0835`, `0843`), the legality test generates on copies with the check and en-passant bytes read from the game record and its list must equal the root's own, move for move (`EngineRoot.root_lists`, `src/engine_root.zig`); every golden replay test runs it (`normal2.jsonl` holds 37 searches after a check), and `src/engine_legality_test.zig` counts it over 481 golden positions with the fixes off and on (61 roots in check, none different). Before the fix (the build of `main` at `18e97dc`): `bestmove 0000` at A1 and A2, every reply refused, F's line cut to `b1d2`. `tools/scripts/uci_protocol_test.py`, session "in check with many moves"; `tools/scripts/uci_stress.py`, session `checks-many` (`docs/ROBUSTNESS.md`).

## DEV-067: Illegal root moves are searched when a legal root move pre-scores below -20500

- **Class:** C (logic bug), whose consequence in the original is class B (an illegal move played). **Status:** implemented, 2026-11-22 (owner decision 42: "Fix it on main"). Switch `dev067_illegal_root_sentinel` (`pos.Fixes`), off in `Fixes.original`, on in `Fixes.corrected`.
- **Original:** main's pre-scoring (`0000:086E`–`1061`) makes each root move and, if the mover's king is attacked after it (`0A13`), gives it the score -20500 (`0A1A mov si,0AFECh`; the White mirror jumps there from `0B76`) and takes one from the legal count (`0A1D dec byte [A518]`); the score is stored at `DG:8D0A + 2i` (`0E3C`). `sort_root_moves` (`0000:668E`, called from `1388` and from the iteration loop at `16E1`) sorts the list by these scores, descending. root_search (`0000:22C0`) puts the first entry in `[A36C]` and searches the first `[A518]` entries only. So an illegal move is kept out of the search only by sorting last. A legal quiet move's pre-score is the static evaluation (`0A78`–`0CCF` with Black to move after it, `0BCA`–`0CCF` with White), which has no floor: with the mover about five queens down it falls below -20500, the legal move sorts after the illegal ones, and an illegal move is searched and played. `set_predicted_reply` (`38C0:0D22`) scores the opponent's replies the same way (`38C0:0E1A mov word [bp-2],0AFECh`, `0E1F dec word [75C4]`; stored through `[bp-8]` = 8D0Ah, `0DD3`) and sorts them with the same routine; the predicted reply, the ponder move the engine sends, is the first entry when the search left no reply of its own (`10E7`–`10FC`).
- **Seen in the original** (the oracle, Fixed depth, auto-play; `tests/golden/dev067.jsonl`): `1K4k1/2nqq3/8/7q/8/2q1q3/2q5/r5q1 w`: the root list b8c7, b8a8, b8c8, b8a7 at -20500 and b8b7, the only legal move, at -21851; the original played Kb8xc7 (2 nodes, 7 makes at depth 1; 2 nodes, 11 makes at depth 3). On the DOS program's screen the next turn wrote "b8c7" for Black, the white king vanished from the board and the status line read "Stalemate!!". `b7/Kn6/6b1/2rq1q2/3q4/1n3kr1/5q2/2q5 w` (four legal moves, pre-scores -20526 to -21438): it played a7b7, illegal (2 nodes, 7 and 11 makes). The boundary, `1K4k1/2nqq3/8/7q/8/2q1q3/2q5/r7 w` (six queens: every legal pre-score above -20500): b8b7, 52 nodes, 56 makes. `mchess-original` gives the same root lists, moves, nodes, makes and line hashes; its legality guard (§9.3 item 3) then refuses the move and plays the first legal move of the root list, with `info string illegal move`.
- **Evidence of intent:** the score and the decrement of `[A518]` are made together, and root_search's only bound on the moves it searches is `[A518]` after a sort by these scores: the sentinel is meant to put every illegal move after every legal one. -20500 lies below every score the search gives (mates are within ±20000; a fail-low entry is -20000 - i). The assumption fails only because the static pre-score has no floor.
- **Every reader of the scores** (the listings searched for `8D0A`, its base `-72F6`/`-72F8` and every constant address in `8D0A`–`8DD1`): the stores at `0000:0E3C` (pre-scoring), `0F34` (-20000 on the exclusion-list path, not modelled and not reached by the UCI engine), `2985` and `2C96` (root_search: a searched entry's score or -20000 - i, only for i < `[A518]`; root_search never reads the array); `sort_root_moves`, `0000:66D6`–`674A`, which only compares (`cmp [bx-72F8h], ax`) and moves entries; and in `set_predicted_reply` the store through `[bp-8]` and the one read, `38C0:10E1 cmp ax,[8D0A]`, the top entry after the sort, made when `[AE36]` is set and a legal reply exists (`[75C4]` non-zero). Wherever every legal score is above -20500, every comparison the sort makes has the same outcome with -20500 and with -32768 (legal against illegal: greater either way; illegal against illegal: equal; legal against legal: unchanged), so the permutation is the same, the legal entries keep their scores, and the top entry read at `10E1` is legal either way. Nothing else reads an illegal entry's score: after the first iteration only legal entries are re-scored, and they stay above -20500.
- **Trigger:** a legal root move (or reply) whose static pre-score is -20500 or less with at least one illegal move beside it. Sampled in the root-cause analysis: a full army against a bare king reaches -18,366 at the lowest; four queens and the other pieces -19,852; five to seven queens trigger it in about one position in five; never in ordinary material. No position was found in which the predicted reply reaches it (the search's own reply is predicted whenever it has one): 640 searches of positions built for it through `mchess-original`, none.
- **Fix** (`pos.rootListScore`, `src/position.zig`; used at the store of `rootprep.prescore`, `0E3C`, and of `gamemove.setPredictedReplyFixes`, after `0E1A`): an illegal move's stored score is -32768 in place of -20500, and, so that the rule is total, a legal score of exactly -32768 (reachable only by 16-bit wrap) is stored as -32767. Every legal move then sorts before every illegal one. The pre-scoring's other uses of the score (the best and second-best, the immediate-mate test) are unchanged.
- **Behaviour change where the original worked:** none. Measured (`src/engine_fixes_test.zig`, `MCHESS_MEASURE_DEV067`) over the 416 searches of the golden files and `deep.jsonl`: the switch alone against the original, and the shipped engine against the shipped engine without it, differ in 0 moves, 0 node counts, 0 scores, 0 make counts and 0 line hashes. The boundary position is searched as the original searches it. The five node counts of the UCI check are unchanged.
- **With the fix, in the original's faulty positions** (the shipped engine): b8b7 at both depths (2 nodes and 7 makes at depth 1; 14 nodes, 23 makes at depth 3); a7a8 (5 nodes at depth 1, 2,838 at depth 3). The guard does not fire.
- **Test:** `src/engine_dev067_test.zig`: with every fix off the engine equals the original on all six records of `tests/golden/dev067.jsonl` (the move the search gave, named by the guard, nodes, makes, line hash); with the shipped fixes, and with the switch alone, the two faulty positions give a legal move with no `illegal move` line and the boundary gives b8b7 with 52 nodes, 56 makes and the original's line; `set_predicted_reply` with replies scored by hand (the four illegal king moves of the first position and b8b7 at -21851): with the fix off b8c7 is predicted, with it on b8b7, and at -20499 both predict b8b7. ## Rule: every uninitialised-memory bug is fixed

Decided by the owner on 2026-11-17, reversing an earlier decision to keep DEV-003. Wherever the original reads memory it never wrote, the rewrite uses the value the code evidently meant. The search is then a function of the position and the settings alone: it no longer changes with the load address or with timer ticks. DEV-003 and DEV-015 are the two found so far; any found later are fixed the same way.

**The fidelity baseline for these is the corrected original.** Class C fixes are normally tested with the fix off, against the unmodified original (AGENTS.md §9.2). That cannot be done here: with these bugs in, the original's behaviour is an accident of memory layout that a rewrite cannot reproduce. So the oracle records the original with the intended values written into those bytes just before they are read, and the golden files are that. With the corrections applied, the golden file is identical at two machine speeds, with the clock running or held, and at two load addresses.

## DEV-003: The move generator orders captures by uninitialised stack bytes

- **Class:** C (logic bug: uninitialised read). **Status:** implemented, 2026-11-18: `movegen.Fixes` in `src/movegen.zig`, with a test showing the original and corrected scores ordering two pawn captures differently. Also corrected in the oracle and, behind a switch, in the model.
- **Original:** `gen_all_moves` (`1E9D:3938`) has four byte locals that it reads once each and never writes: `[bp-19h]` at `3A40`, `[bp-1Fh]` at `403A`, `[bp-21h]` at `417E` and `[bp-23h]` at `4808`. Each is added to the order score of a pawn or king capture. The list is then sorted by score, so the order in which those captures are searched depends on whatever earlier code left in those stack bytes.
- **Effect:** the search tree depends on the stack's history. It changes with the address the program is loaded at (3 of 7 test searches changed when the load segment moved from 1010 to 1810), and with every timer tick, because the interrupt handler pushes registers onto the same stack. The same search made 20,806 moves at one machine speed and 20,811 at another. The move played was the same in every case seen. It cannot crash: the byte is only ever a sort key. In 943 reads over seven test searches the byte was 00 in 59% and FF in 28%.
- **Evidence of intent:** at all four sites the code loads the move's mark (`DG:781A[...]`) into AL, overwrites AL at once with `pop ax`, and then adds the never-written local. The local was meant to hold the mark just loaded; the store is missing. Found independently by two investigations (`docs/arch/notes/clock-effect.md`, `docs/arch/21-move-generation.md`).
- **Trigger:** any full-width node that generates a pawn or king capture.
- **Fix:** each of the four terms is the move's mark, `DG:781A[word[DG:1A80 + 2·from] + to]`. In the model the switch is `FIX_DEV003_STRAY_SCORE` in `tools/research/movegen/model.py`, off by default so the model still matches the unmodified original. In the oracle it is on by default (`--uninit corrected`).
- **Behaviour change where the original worked:** none can be defined; the original's behaviour here is not determined by the position. Against one run of the unmodified original, all seven test searches made a different number of moves; the move played was the same in each.
- **Test:** the oracle's golden files, made with the correction, regenerate identically at two speeds, two clock modes and two load addresses. A direct test of the model's corrected path against the corrected original is still to be written, with the port of `gen_all_moves`.
- **The oracle's first correction was not this one (found 2026-11-19).** Until then the oracle wrote `[781A + [bp-0Ch] + to]` at every site. At the two pawn-capture sites (`1E9D:3A40`, `417E`) `[bp-0Ch]` is not yet written in that call, because pawns come first in the slot order `DG:1A60`, so files recorded that way still depended on a stale word. The engine joined to the real routines showed it: four of five check-evasion subtrees differed until the oracle wrote the move's own mark. `oracle.py --dev003 move` writes the specified value; 7 of the 10 smoke searches then make different trees, with the same moves played. Every golden file was re-recorded with it (2026-11-19; it is the default of oracle version 4); a file recorded with it says `"dev003": "move"` in its headers.

## DEV-042: Clearing the hash tables leaves the last 2,048 bytes uncleared

- **Class:** C (logic bug: uninitialised read). **Status:** it falls under the rule above, so it is fixed: the port clears the whole table (`HashTable.clear` in `src/searchnode.zig`). `hashsize.unclearedTail` gives the range for a test of the original behaviour. ## Platform adaptations (class F), to be detailed as each is designed

- **DEV-F01: No copy protection.** The CD-ROM "recharge" check on `M_CHESS8.PRO` and the system date is not ported.
- **DEV-F02: No learning.** `LEARNW.M80` and `LEARNB.M80` are neither read nor written (owner decision, 2026-11-17).

- **DEV-F05: UCI time management is Juggernaut's** (owner decision 20, 2026-11-19; implemented). A timed `go` runs the original's Fixed depth level at depth 30, and the time control of the owner's engine Juggernaut (`src/timecontrol.zig`, a port of `uChessTimeControls.pas`) decides when to stop. None of the original's time management runs in UCI play: not its budget, its stop tests, its fail-low extension, its easy-move rule, its after-book bonus, nor the small hash tables it uses at fast controls. Inside an iteration the stop is the original's stop flag, as DEV-F08, tested every 1024th node of the search and at the iteration loop's first node. At the end of an iteration the loop's own depth limit `DG:64` is lowered to that depth + 1, so a search stopped after iteration k equals `go depth k` on the move, nodes, makes and line hash. Elapsed time runs from the moment the `go` line is read. Design: `docs/UCI_TIME.md`. Tests: `src/timecontrol_test.zig` (the unit against a transcription of the Pascal, 259,401 calls), `src/engine_time_test.zig` (a scripted clock), the protocol test; a first safety run of 400 fast games with no time loss, and 12,980 games from 2026-11-20 to 22, from half a second for the game to 3+2 and 40 moves in 60 s, pondering on and off, with no crash or illegal move once the two faults they found were fixed, and one time loss at 40 moves in 60 s on the last move before the control (`docs/UCI_TIME.md` §6). **A third fault of Juggernaut's time control is corrected in this copy** (2026-11-22, under decision 26, as the two of 2026-11-20 were; switch `Fixes.clock_reserve` in `src/timecontrol.zig`, on in the engine): the Pascal lets `panic` and `max` reach the whole clock but twice the Move Overhead, so with one move to go, or an increment large against the clock, a move could use all but 60 ms, and a game at 40 moves in 60 s was lost on time on the last move before the control. Now neither exceeds the clock less a fifth of it (and less at least the Move Overhead); the target, `movetime` and every limit already inside that reserve are unchanged (61 of 270 cells of a grid change, `docs/UCI_TIME.md` §2). With eight other searching engines on the machine, 200 × `go wtime 3000 btime 3000 movestogo 1` on varied positions left at least 16 ms on the clock before and 566 ms after. **Score unit and promotions** (2026-11-22, review findings 4 and 5, owner decision 41): Juggernaut's margins are centipawns (StableScore's 25, `ROOT_TROUBLE_MARGIN` 50) and MChess's scores count a pawn as 200, so until this date they were applied at half their size (root trouble on a fall of 25 centipawns; stable only within 12.5). The time control now has a score unit, 2 in the engine (`TimeControl.score_unit`; 1 for the Pascal's recorded vectors, which replay unchanged): the margins are 50 and 100 raw, exact. And a root move's key for the clock carries its promotion piece (`Reporter.rootMoveKey`), so f7f8r at one iteration and f7f8q at the next are a change of best move; an expected move is not formed when the opponent's move was a promotion or the predicted answer is a pawn's move to its last rank, since a PV word holds no piece.
